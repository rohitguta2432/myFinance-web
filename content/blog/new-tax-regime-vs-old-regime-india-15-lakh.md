88% of Indian taxpayers have moved to the new tax regime ([Business Standard, citing CBDT](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/cbdt-to-issue-new-tax-rules-88-taxpayers-already-in-new-regime-126020500198_1.html)). Most of them never ran the math.

For a ₹15 lakh+ earner, picking the wrong regime costs ₹40,000 to ₹1,60,000 every year. That isn't a typo. Across a 25-year career, that's ₹10–40 lakh of pure choice cost — money lost to inertia, not to bad markets.

Your CA filed your returns. Payroll picked a regime for TDS. You signed Form 12BB. Nobody actually ran the break-even.

This is the new tax regime vs old regime decision most ₹15L+ professionals have outsourced — and most have outsourced wrong.

## Default ≠ optimal

Since FY 2023-24, the new tax regime is the default for individuals, HUFs and AOPs ([Income Tax Department, AY 2026-27 page](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1)). To stay in the old regime, you have to actively opt out — at the time of filing your ITR, or via Form 10-IEA if you have business or professional income.

Most salaried employees don't opt out. Their employer's payroll runs new-regime TDS unless they tick a box. By the time the ITR is filed, the regime is effectively decided by inertia, not analysis.

That's not a tax strategy. That's a default.

The new regime is genuinely the right answer for most salaried earners up to about ₹12.75 lakh. Above that line, the answer depends entirely on what deductions you can actually claim — and "actually claim" is the part most people get wrong.

## The slabs nobody compares correctly

Here are the FY 2025-26 slabs (you'll file these in AY 2026-27):

| Income slab | New regime | Old regime |
|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹2.5L | Nil | Nil |
| ₹2.5L–₹4L | Nil | 5% |
| ₹4L–₹5L | 5% | 5% |
| ₹5L–₹8L | 5% | 20% |
| ₹8L–₹10L | 10% | 20% |
| ₹10L–₹12L | 10% | 30% |
| ₹12L–₹16L | 15% | 30% |
| ₹16L–₹20L | 20% | 30% |
| ₹20L–₹24L | 25% | 30% |
| Above ₹24L | 30% | 30% |

Source: [Income Tax Department, AY 2026-27](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1).

The new regime gives you a flatter, gentler curve from ₹4L to ₹24L. The old regime flips you straight to 30% above ₹10L of taxable income. That looks brutal — until you remember the old regime lets you subtract HRA, 80C, NPS, mediclaim and home loan interest before that 30% bites.

The real comparison isn't slab-vs-slab. It's *post-deduction taxable income* under each regime.

## The ₹12.75 lakh cliff: where new regime wins by default

Two numbers do almost all the work in the new regime:

- Standard deduction: **₹75,000** (vs ₹50,000 in the old regime).
- Section 87A rebate: **up to ₹60,000**, wiping out tax on income up to ₹12 lakh ([Budget 2025 FAQs, Income Tax Department](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Documents/Budget/budget-2025/faqs-budget-2025.pdf)).

Stack them. A salaried employee earning up to **₹12.75 lakh** pays zero income tax under the new regime, before claiming a single deduction.

If your CTC is below this line, the new tax regime vs old regime debate is over. You're done. Don't buy an LIC endowment policy in March to "save tax." There's no tax to save.

Above ₹12.75L, the rebate evaporates and slabs kick in fully. This is where the comparison actually starts mattering — and where most ₹15L+ earners stop reading and outsource the decision.

## The ₹15L–₹24L middle zone: where deductions decide everything

This is where 70% of the MyFinancial audience sits. And this is where the wrong regime quietly compounds into lakhs.

ClearTax has computed the break-even — the amount of legitimate deductions you need to claim for the old regime to beat the new regime ([ClearTax old vs new regime](https://cleartax.in/s/old-tax-regime-vs-new-tax-regime)):

| Total income | Deductions needed for old regime to win |
|---|---|
| ₹15,00,000 | More than **₹5,43,750** |
| ₹20,00,000 | More than **₹7,08,330** |
| ₹25,00,000 | More than **₹8,00,000** |

Now stop and ask: do you actually claim ₹5.4 lakh of deductions?

Here's what ₹5.4L looks like, fully stacked, for a ₹15L earner:

- Section 80C (EPF + ELSS + PPF + LIC): ₹1,50,000
- Section 80CCD(1B) NPS: ₹50,000
- Section 80D health insurance (self + senior parents): ₹75,000
- HRA exemption (rent ₹35,000/month, basic ₹6,00,000, metro): ~₹2,40,000
- Section 24(b) home loan interest on self-occupied: up to ₹2,00,000
- Standard deduction (old regime): ₹50,000

Total: **₹7,65,000**.

You only hit the break-even if you genuinely pay rent (with proof), max NPS, max mediclaim including parents, and run a home loan. That's a real upper-middle-class Bangalore or Bombay profile.

Most ₹15L earners don't have this. They have ₹1.5L of EPF + ELSS, ₹25K of mediclaim, no HRA because they live with parents, no home loan. Real deductions: ₹2L. They're ₹3.4L below break-even.

For that profile, the new regime saves ₹40,000–₹70,000 a year vs the old regime. The "tax-saving" LIC premium they bought to push 80C higher is irrelevant — they shouldn't be in the old regime to begin with.

## Above ₹24L: where the old regime quietly returns to favour

Above ₹24 lakh of taxable income, both regimes hit you at 30%. Each rupee of legitimate deduction is now worth 30 paise to you. Suddenly the old regime looks attractive again — *if* you have the deductions.

For a ₹40 lakh CTC professional with the full deduction stack:

- HRA exemption: ₹4,00,000
- 80C: ₹1,50,000
- 80CCD(1B) NPS: ₹50,000
- 80D (self + parents): ₹75,000
- 24(b) home loan interest: ₹2,00,000
- Standard deduction: ₹50,000

Total old-regime deductions: **₹9,25,000**.

Old regime taxable: ₹40L − ₹9.25L = ₹30.75L → tax roughly ₹7,35,000.
New regime taxable: ₹40L − ₹0.75L = ₹39.25L → tax roughly ₹7,87,500.

Old regime saves ~₹52,500/year. Over a 15-year career, ₹7.9 lakh of pure choice cost — before compounding the tax saved into investments.

One detail almost nobody flags: **employer NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2)** is allowed in *both* regimes ([Income Tax Department](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1)). If your employer offers a 10% NPS contribution and you haven't opted in, you're leaving deduction on the table regardless of which regime you pick.

## Real numbers: Priya, 36, senior product manager

Priya works at a Bangalore SaaS company. CTC ₹38L. Pays ₹50,000/month rent in HSR. Has a let-out flat in Kanakapura on a ₹35L home loan. Maxes EPF, NPS, parents' mediclaim.

Her old regime number:
- HRA exemption (~₹4,40,000), 80C ₹1,50,000, 80CCD(1B) ₹50,000, 80D ₹75,000, 24(b) ₹2,00,000, standard deduction ₹50,000.
- Total deductions: ~₹9,65,000. Taxable: ₹28,35,000. Tax (incl 4% cess): ~₹6,90,000.

Her new regime number:
- Standard deduction ₹75,000. Taxable: ₹37,25,000. Tax (incl 4% cess): ~₹7,25,000.

Old regime saves Priya ~₹35,000 a year. Her CA never ran this number — he just filed whatever Form 12BB she submitted. She'd been in the new regime by default for two years. That's ₹70,000 of foregone tax savings, before compounding.

Move the same Priya to a ₹50L role next year, keep the deductions intact, and the old-regime advantage widens to ~₹75,000 a year.

## 5 mistakes ₹15L+ earners keep making on the regime question

**1. Letting payroll TDS pick the regime, then never overriding.** Cost: ₹30,000–₹1,00,000/year if you should have been in the old regime. Tell HR your choice in April — not in February when it's too late.

**2. Counting LIC endowment premium as "tax saved" without computing the post-tax IRR.** Endowment policies return 4–5% IRR (we covered this in [The LIC Endowment Trap](/blog/lic-endowment-policy-returns-trap)). PPF returns ~7%, NPS Tier 1 long-term ~9–11%. The "tax saving" on a bad product isn't tax saving — it's a worse product wrapped in 80C paper.

**3. Forgetting Form 10-IEA if you have any business or freelance income.** Salaried-only filers can flip regimes annually in their ITR. Anyone with business or professional income must file Form 10-IEA to opt out, and **the option to switch back to the new regime is available only once in a lifetime** ([Income Tax Department](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1)). Most consultants and Substack-side-income professionals miss this entirely.

**4. Claiming HRA when you didn't actually pay rent.** Cost: ₹50,000+ if you get a notice. ITR scrutiny on rent receipts has tightened — landlord PAN is mandatory above ₹1L/year of rent. Either you actually pay or you don't. There's no third option.

**5. Switching regimes annually on vibes instead of math.** Your salary structure should be designed *around* the regime — HRA-heavy + home loan = old regime; lean salary + ESOPs = new regime. Picking retroactively means you've already paid for deductions you can't claim.

## What to do (high level, no products)

1. Compute your *real* deductions — receipts, not aspirations. Be honest about HRA, mediclaim, home loan interest.
2. Compare against the break-even for your income bracket above. If you're more than ₹50,000 below break-even, stay in the new regime and stop buying tax-saving products.
3. If you're above break-even, opt out properly: ITR-level for salaried; Form 10-IEA for business income.
4. Re-run this every April. Slab structures shift. Your salary, rent and loan interest all move. The regime that wins this year may lose next year.
5. If you have employer NPS, opt in. It's the one deduction that survives in both regimes.

## FAQ

**Which tax regime is better for ₹15 lakh salary?**
Depends on your deductions. New regime wins unless you can claim more than **₹5,43,750** in legitimate deductions. For most salaried ₹15L earners without HRA + home loan + maxed NPS, new regime saves ₹40K–₹70K a year.

**How much deduction do I need to claim for old regime to beat new regime at ₹20 lakh?**
About **₹7,08,330**. That requires HRA + 80C max + 80CCD(1B) + 80D + 24(b) home loan interest. If you have all five, old regime wins. Three of five — new regime wins.

**Can I switch between old and new tax regime every year?**
Yes, if you have only salary or pension income — you toggle in your ITR by the section 139(1) due date. No, if you have business or professional income — you opt out via Form 10-IEA, but you only get **one chance** in your lifetime to switch back to the new regime.

**Is HRA allowed in the new tax regime?**
No. HRA, LTA, 80C (PPF/ELSS/LIC), 80D (mediclaim), 80CCD(1B) (self-NPS) and home loan interest on self-occupied property are all disallowed in the new regime. The standard deduction (₹75,000) and employer NPS contribution under 80CCD(2) are the two notable exceptions.

**What is the ₹12 lakh tax-free limit in the new regime?**
The Section 87A rebate is up to ₹60,000 in the new regime — exactly enough to wipe out the tax on ₹12 lakh of income. Salaried earners get the standard deduction on top, so income up to **₹12.75 lakh** is tax-free.
