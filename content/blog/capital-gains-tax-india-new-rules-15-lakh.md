## Capital gains tax India: what the new rules actually changed

The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 rewrote capital gains taxation with effect from 23 July 2024 ([Income Tax Department, AY 2026-27](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1)).

Three rate changes that matter:

- **LTCG on equity** moved from 10% to **12.5%** ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-ltcg-tax))
- **STCG on equity** moved from 15% to **20%** under Section 111A ([Income Tax India tutorial](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/tutorials/14-%20stcg.pdf))
- **Indexation benefit removed** for most assets, replaced by a flat 12.5% LTCG rate without indexation

The annual exemption on equity LTCG was raised from ₹1 lakh to ₹1.25 lakh — a small win. Everything else got more expensive.

Budget 2025 and Budget 2026 made no changes ([TaxGuru, Income Tax Act 2025](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/capital-gains-income-tax-act-2025-tax-period-2026-27.html)). These rules are now your reality for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) and FY 2026-27.

## Four asset classes, four different math problems

Most people treat "capital gains" as one rule. It isn't. The new regime cleanly splits your portfolio into four buckets, each with different rates and holding periods.

| Asset | Holding period for LTCG | LTCG rate | STCG rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listed equity, equity MF | 12 months | 12.5% above ₹1.25L | 20% |
| Real estate (post 23-Jul-24) | 24 months | 12.5% no indexation | Slab rate |
| Gold, gold ETF | 24 months | 12.5% | Slab rate |
| Debt MF (post 1-Apr-23) | N/A | Slab rate | Slab rate |

That last row is the one most professionals still get wrong. Debt mutual funds purchased on or after 1 April 2023 don't qualify for LTCG treatment at all — every rupee of gain is taxed at your slab rate, regardless of how long you held ([ClearTax LTCG guide](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-ltcg-tax)). For a ₹15L+ earner in the 30% bracket, that's a 30% tax on debt fund gains, not 12.5%.

If your CA still tells you debt funds are "tax-efficient at LTCG rates," your CA is filing under rules that expired three years ago.

## The ₹1.25 lakh exemption nobody actually uses

LTCG on listed equity and equity mutual funds is exempt up to ₹1.25 lakh **per financial year** ([ClearTax LTCG guide](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-ltcg-tax)). Most people read this and move on. They shouldn't.

This isn't a one-time benefit. It's an annual reset. Every March, you can harvest up to ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains, pay zero tax, and reset your cost basis upward. If you don't use it, it disappears.

A ₹50 lakh equity portfolio earning 12% a year throws off ₹6 lakh of unrealised gains annually. Skip the harvest, hold for ten years, sell — your taxable LTCG is ₹60L+ minus one ₹1.25L exemption. Harvest annually, you've reset ₹12.5 lakh of basis along the way and your final taxable gain is dramatically lower.

The catch: harvest means actually selling and rebuying. Same scrip, same date, gets treated as a wash by your broker but the income tax department recognises the realised gain. Most people don't do it because it feels like activity for nothing. The tax saving over 25 years is ₹3-6 lakh on a ₹50L portfolio.

## The grandfathering choice property owners get exactly once

If you bought property before 23 July 2024 and sell it after, you can choose ([ClearTax LTCG guide](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-ltcg-tax)):

- **20% LTCG with indexation** (old rule), or
- **12.5% LTCG without indexation** (new rule)

This choice exists only for resident individuals and HUFs, only for land or building bought before 23 July 2024. Future buyers don't get it.

Which is better depends on how long you've held and how fast property prices ran. A flat bought in 2008 in a Bangalore tier-1 area where indexed cost has compounded heavily often does better at 20% with indexation. A flat bought in 2020 with modest price appreciation usually does better at 12.5% without indexation.

This isn't a rule of thumb you can apply blind. A ₹2 crore sale where indexation pulls cost from ₹80L to ₹1.4 crore taxes ₹60L at 20% = ₹12L. Same sale at 12.5% without indexation taxes ₹1.2 crore at 12.5% = ₹15L. Indexation wins by ₹3 lakh.

Same ₹2 crore sale where indexation only pulls cost from ₹1.4 crore to ₹1.6 crore — indexation taxes ₹40L at 20% = ₹8L. Flat 12.5% on ₹60L = ₹7.5L. New rule wins by ₹50,000.

The point: this is a one-shot decision worth lakhs. Most CAs default-pick whichever is easier to file. Run both numbers.

## STCG went from 15% to 20% — and most active investors haven't priced it in

The Section 111A rate jump from 15% to 20% applies to listed equity, equity mutual funds and business trust units — anything that pays STT and is sold inside 12 months ([Income Tax India tutorial](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/tutorials/14-%20stcg.pdf)).

For someone who churns ₹10L of trades a year at, say, ₹2L of net short-term gains, the tax went from ₹30,000 to ₹40,000. Five percent of net gains. Not catastrophic on its own, but combined with the 12.5% LTCG bump, it makes one thing very clear: India's tax system now rewards sitting still more than it used to.

If your portfolio churn is more habit than thesis, the new STCG rate is a 33% tax on that habit.

## Real numbers: a 35-year-old IT professional, ₹50L equity portfolio

Rohit is 35, earns ₹28L salary, has a ₹50 lakh equity mutual fund portfolio bought five years ago at an average cost of ₹30 lakh. Total unrealised LTCG: ₹20 lakh. He decides to sell ₹20 lakh worth this year for a home renovation.

**The way most ₹15L+ earners file it:**

- ₹20L sale × (₹20L gain / ₹50L value) = ₹8L realised LTCG
- Minus ₹1.25L exemption = ₹6.75L taxable
- 12.5% × ₹6.75L = **₹84,375 tax**

**The way it could have been filed if he'd planned:**

- Same total sale, but harvested ₹1.25L gains every year for the previous five years (₹6.25L of basis already reset, zero tax)
- Realised LTCG this year: ₹4.5L instead of ₹8L
- Minus ₹1.25L exemption = ₹3.25L taxable
- 12.5% × ₹3.25L = **₹40,625 tax**

Difference: **₹43,750 in one year**. Across his career, this single habit is worth ₹8-12 lakh.

He didn't lose this money to bad markets. He lost it to never running the capital gains math.

## Five mistakes that cost ₹15L+ earners real money

**1. Treating debt MFs as tax-efficient.** Anything bought after 1 April 2023 is taxed at slab rate, not LTCG. For a 30% bracket professional, that's a 30% tax bill on every rupee of gain. Cost: ₹50K-2L per year on a ₹20L debt allocation.

**2. Skipping annual ₹1.25 lakh LTCG harvesting.** Resetting cost basis is free money. Five missed harvests on a ₹50L portfolio = ₹3-6L extra tax at exit.

**3. Defaulting on the property indexation choice.** Picking the wrong option on a ₹2 crore property sale costs ₹2-5L. Most CAs don't run both calculations.

**4. Forgetting STCG bumped to 20%.** If you took short-term equity gains in FY 2024-25, your tax went up by a third versus what you remembered. Cost: 5% of trading gains, every year.

**5. Locking gains in 54EC bonds without doing the math.** Section 54EC bonds give a long-term capital gain exemption up to ₹50 lakh, locked for five years, paying around 5.25% ([ClearTax 54EC guide](https://cleartax.in/s/section-54ec-bonds)). At 12.5% LTCG, paying the tax and investing the after-tax amount in a balanced portfolio at 9-10% post-tax beats the 54EC return for most ₹15L+ earners. Cost of getting this wrong: ₹3-8L over five years on a ₹50L gain.

## What to do about capital gains tax — without being told what to buy

Three behaviours, no product picks:

- **Harvest LTCG every March.** Sell up to ₹1.25 lakh of gains, rebuy the same fund the next day. Reset basis. Do it on autopilot.
- **Map your debt holdings by purchase date.** Anything from before 1 April 2023 still gets old LTCG rules. Anything after is slab. Treat them as different products.
- **Run both columns on any property sale.** 20% with indexation vs 12.5% flat. Whichever is lower wins. This is a calculator question, not a CA question.

If your portfolio is over ₹25 lakh and you've never explicitly asked these questions, you're almost certainly losing 0.5-1.5% of portfolio value to tax inefficiency every year. On a 25-year horizon, that's a 15-30% reduction in your final corpus.

## FAQ

**What is the new LTCG tax rate in India for AY 2026-27?**
Long-term capital gains on listed equity and equity mutual funds are taxed at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per year. Other assets — real estate, gold, unlisted equity — are taxed at 12.5% without indexation. Rates are unchanged from AY 2025-26.

**Is indexation still available for property in 2025?**
Only as a grandfathered choice. If you bought land or building before 23 July 2024 and sell after that date, you can pick either 20% with indexation or 12.5% without. For purchases after 23 July 2024, indexation is gone.

**How is STCG on equity taxed after Budget 2024?**
Short-term capital gains on listed equity, equity mutual funds and business trust units are taxed at 20% under Section 111A, up from 15%. Effective from 23 July 2024.

**What is the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption?**
An annual exemption that applies only to long-term capital gains on listed equity, equity mutual funds and business trust units. Up to ₹1.25 lakh of such gains in a financial year are tax-free. The exemption resets every 1 April.

**How do Section 54F and 54EC reduce capital gains tax?**
Section 54F exempts LTCG on the sale of any long-term asset (other than residential property) if you reinvest in a new residential house within prescribed timelines. Section 54EC exempts LTCG up to ₹50 lakh from sale of land or building if invested in NHAI/REC/PFC/IRFC bonds within six months, locked for five years ([ClearTax 54EC guide](https://cleartax.in/s/section-54ec-bonds)).
