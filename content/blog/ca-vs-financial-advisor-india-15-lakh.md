Your CA shaved ₹40,000 off last year's tax bill. Nobody noticed the ₹40 lakh hole in your retirement plan.

That isn't your CA's fault. It is your problem.

For ₹15 lakh+ earners, the most expensive financial mistake isn't a bad fund or a bad year. It's the assumption that "I have a CA" means "I have financial planning." Those are two different jobs, two different regulators, and two completely different time horizons. Most professionals never notice the gap until they're 50 and the math has already happened.

This post is the ca vs financial advisor india question — answered with numbers, not opinions.

## You don't have a tax problem. You have an outsourcing problem.

Here is the part nobody says out loud.

A Chartered Accountant in India is licensed and trained for compliance: filing ITRs, audits, GST, ROC filings, transfer pricing, IT Act and Companies Act work. The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) regulates that scope under the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 ([ICAI](https://www.icai.org/post.html?post_id=2)).

A SEBI Registered Investment Adviser (RIA) is licensed and trained for something else: giving you written, fee-based investment advice — across asset classes — under the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013 ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/jul-2020/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-investment-advisers-regulations-2013-last-amended-on-july-3-2020-_47165.html)).

Two different statutes. Two different exams. Two different mandates.

When you ask your CA "what should I do with my portfolio?" you are asking a tax-compliance specialist a financial-planning question. Sometimes the answer is right. The structure says it shouldn't be the default.

## What your CA is actually paid to do

Look at what your CA's invoice covers. It is some subset of:

- File your ITR-1/2/3 by July 31
- Maximise deductions allowable under the Income Tax Act, 1961
- Reconcile Form 26AS, AIS, TIS with your return
- Respond to scrutiny notices, faceless assessments, refund delays
- TDS/advance-tax planning for the current financial year
- For business owners: GST, audit, ROC, MCA filings

Time horizon: 12 months. Source of truth: the IT Act. Optimisation target: this year's tax outgo.

That is a real, hard, valuable job. ₹15L+ earners absolutely need it done well.

It is also, by design, a one-year compliance lens. Not a 30-year planning lens.

## What your CA is not paid to do

These are the questions that cost ₹15L+ earners the most money — and almost none of them sit inside your CA's licensure:

1. **Are you under-insured?** Most ₹15L+ earners need 15–20× annual income as term cover ([IRDAI Annual Report 2022-23](https://irdai.gov.in/annual-reports)). Your CA does not run that calculation.
2. **Do your 6 mutual funds actually own different stocks?** Portfolio overlap of 60–80% is normal in Indian large-cap-heavy folios. Your CA does not check.
3. **Is your ULIP / endowment / money-back policy a trap?** Many traditional plans return 4–5.5% IRR over 20 years ([IRDAI](https://irdai.gov.in)). Your CA can see the 80C deduction but isn't asked to compute the IRR.
4. **What corpus do you actually need at 60, in today's rupees?** That is a 25-year inflation-adjusted projection. Not in scope.
5. **Are your nominees, joint holdings, and will set up correctly?** Estate planning sits with a lawyer, not your CA.
6. **Is your asset allocation drifting from your risk capacity?** Annual rebalance discipline. Not in scope.

You can ask your CA these questions. Many will give a friendly, off-the-cuff opinion. That is goodwill, not licensure. Under the SEBI RIA framework, only registered advisers can hold themselves out as giving investment advice for consideration ([SEBI Investor Charter for IAs](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/dec-2021/Investor%20Charter_p.pdf)).

The polite version: your CA's job ends where the IT Act ends.

## The ₹40 lakh hole — actual math

Meet a real composite. 34-year-old engineering lead at a Bengaluru product company, ₹35L CTC. Married, one kid, parents 64 and 61. By every visible measure, "well-organised":

- 80C maxed via PPF + ELSS + EPF
- 80D maxed (₹25K self + ₹50K parents)
- NPS Tier 1, ₹50K extra under 80CCD(1B)
- 4 LIC policies bought "for the family" between 2014 and 2019
- 6 equity mutual funds via a regular-plan distributor
- ₹1 cr term cover taken in 2017
- Home loan running, HRA paused
- Files ITR through a CA every year, no notices

His CA is doing a great job on his ITR. Now run the rest of the numbers.

**Insurance gap.** At 17× income, he needs roughly ₹6 cr term cover. He has ₹1 cr. He is under-insured by ₹5 cr. Topping up to ₹5 cr cover at age 34 costs about ₹22,000–₹28,000/year ([Policybazaar Term Insurance Calculator](https://www.policybazaar.com/life-insurance/term-insurance/)) — less than his Saturday Swiggy budget.

**LIC drag.** His 4 endowment policies have a combined annual premium of ₹2.4L and project a maturity IRR of about 4.7% ([LIC Bonus Information](https://licindia.in/bonus-information)). Term + index over the same 16 years would have produced ~3.2× the corpus at the same outflow ([NSE Nifty 50 TRI 20-year CAGR](https://www.niftyindices.com/indices/equity/broad-based-indices/nifty-50)).

**Mutual fund overlap.** His 6 funds — all large-cap-heavy — share roughly 70% of their top-25 holdings. He thinks he is diversified across 6 schemes. He actually owns 3 effective portfolios.

**Regular-plan trail.** ~1.0–1.35% expense gap vs direct plans on his ₹38L equity corpus. Compounded over 25 years at 12% gross, that is the [direct-vs-regular crore-gap](/blog/direct-vs-regular-mutual-funds-india-crore-gap) territory.

**Retirement projection.** At his current SIP of ₹65K/month, retiring at 60 with current lifestyle at 6% inflation requires ₹6.0 cr in today's rupees. His current trajectory lands him at ~₹2.4 cr in today's rupees.

Add the four leaks at present value: ~₹38–42 lakh.

That is the ₹40L hole. None of it is in his ITR. None of it gets surfaced at tax time. None of it is his CA's job.

## Why the ca vs financial advisor india gap is structural

This is the part most ₹15L+ earners miss.

Your CA isn't withholding the truth. The truth was never on their desk. ICAI's [Code of Ethics](https://www.icai.org/post/code-of-ethics) and the SEBI Investment Advisers Regulations together create a clean line: tax-and-audit work on one side, investment advice on the other. A CA giving paid investment advice without separate SEBI registration is operating outside scope.

So when you ask your CA "should I keep this LIC policy?" the legal-best answer is "I'm not the right professional for that question." Most CAs are too polite to say it that way.

The result is a structural gap nobody is paid to close. The ₹15L+ professional fills it themselves on weekends, badly, with three Reddit threads and a friend's hot tip.

## 5 mistakes that turn a ₹40K saving into a ₹40L cost

1. **Asking "what tax can I save?" instead of "where am I 5× exposed?"** Tax saving is a 1-year question. Concentration risk is a 25-year question. The bigger number always wins.
2. **Buying insurance through your CA's network.** Cross-referrals between CAs and insurance agents are common and earn the agent a 25–35% first-year commission ([IRDAI](https://irdai.gov.in/document-detail?documentId=383321)). The product that gets sold is rarely the product that should be bought.
3. **Treating ITR filing season as your annual financial review.** It isn't. It is a backward-looking compliance event. By the time it happens, the year's allocation, insurance, and rebalance decisions are already locked.
4. **Confusing tax-saving with wealth-building.** ELSS, PPF, NPS, life insurance, ULIPs all sit under 80C. Their post-tax 25-year IRRs differ by 4–6 percentage points. The deduction is identical. The compounding outcome is not.
5. **Outsourcing one piece, assuming the other four are handled.** ₹15L+ financial life has five dimensions: cashflow, insurance, investments, tax, retirement. Hiring a CA covers ~1.2 of them.

## What to actually do

No product picks. Just the structure.

- **Keep your CA.** They are doing a job nobody else can do for you. Don't fire them.
- **Separate the two functions in your head.** Tax compliance is not financial planning. Use different people for different jobs.
- **Run a 5-dimension diagnostic at least once a year.** Cashflow, insurance gap, investment overlap, tax efficiency, retirement adequacy. The list is non-negotiable; what you use to run it is your choice.
- **For investment advice, work with a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser** — fee-only is the cleanest model. Verify on the [SEBI RIA list](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=14).
- **Bring your CA the *output* of the planning exercise**, not the other way around. The plan tells you what to buy/sell. Your CA tells you how to file what happened.
- **Run the numbers before you outsource them.** Most ₹15L+ earners discover the gap the moment they see their own diagnostic — it isn't subtle.

## FAQ

**Can a CA give investment advice in India?**
Not for consideration as a regular activity, unless they're separately registered as a SEBI Investment Adviser ([SEBI IA Regulations, 2013](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/jul-2020/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-investment-advisers-regulations-2013-last-amended-on-july-3-2020-_47165.html)). Casual, unpaid opinions sit in a grey zone. Paid advice without registration is non-compliant.

**Is a SEBI RIA different from a CA?**
Yes. Different regulator (SEBI vs ICAI), different exam (NISM-X-A and X-B vs CA Final), different scope (investment advice vs tax/audit). Some professionals hold both qualifications.

**Do I need both a CA and a financial advisor?**
For ₹15L+ earners in India, usually yes. Tax compliance and 30-year planning are different jobs. Some fee-only RIAs bundle tax filing; most don't.

**Why doesn't my CA flag my insurance and portfolio gaps?**
It is outside their licensure and scope of engagement. They aren't paid to model your insurance need or run portfolio overlap. Many will share informal opinions; that is goodwill, not their service contract.

**What does a financial diagnostic actually do?**
At minimum, it scans five dimensions: cashflow stability, insurance adequacy, investment quality and overlap, tax efficiency across long horizons, and retirement corpus adequacy in inflation-adjusted rupees. It surfaces the ₹40L hole most ₹15L+ earners are carrying without realising.
